# Versión Testigo — Style guide (v0.1, proposed)

Every decision here is a **proposal** until the review committee signs it off.
The drafting step is given this file verbatim, so changing it changes every
future draft. Record changes in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## 1. Source texts

- Old Testament: Westminster Leningrad Codex (public domain), read word by
  word with Strong's numbers and morphology.
- New Testament: SBL Greek New Testament (CC BY 4.0).
- Where the Hebrew has a *ketiv/qere* or a well-known textual question, follow
  the Masoretic reading in the text and put the alternative in a note.
- **No published translation is shown to the drafter.** The BSB and others are
  used only afterwards, as checks (see `README.md`).

## 2. Audience and register

- Spanish readers anywhere, with a neutral Latin American base.
- Plural "you" is **ustedes**, never *vosotros*. God is addressed as **tú**.
- Clear contemporary Spanish, read aloud well in church. Avoid archaisms
  (*he aquí*, *empero*) and avoid slang.

## 3. How literal

Moderately formal: keep the Hebrew order of ideas, its images and its repeated
words wherever natural Spanish allows; change structure only when a literal
rendering would mislead or not be Spanish.

- **Keep key repetitions.** If the author repeats a word on purpose, render it
  the same way each time (see the glossary). In Ruth: *volver* (שׁוּב),
  *amor leal* (חֶסֶד), *redentor / redimir* (גָּאַל), *ala* (כָּנָף, 2:12 and
  3:9), *de valía* (חַיִל, 2:1, 3:11, 4:11), *vacía(s)* (רֵיקָם, 1:21, 3:17).
- **Keep idioms that Spanish readers can follow** ("hablar al corazón",
  "hallar favor ante tus ojos") and explain the rest in notes.
- Don't add words that aren't implied. If something must be supplied for
  sense, keep it minimal and note it.

## 5. Proper names

Traditional Spanish spellings (as in Reina-Valera), with accents:
Noemí, Rut, Orfa, Booz, Elimelec, Mahlón, Quelión, Belén, Judá, Moab,
efrateo(s), Efrata, Obed, Isaí, David, Fares, Tamar, Hezrón, Ram, Aminadab,
Naasón, Salmón, Raquel, Lea. Meaningful names get a note (Noemí "agradable",
Mara "amarga", Belén "casa de pan").

## 6. People and gender

Render by context: אִישׁ is *hombre* or *esposo*; בָּנִים are *hijos*;
יְלָדִים *muchachos* where the author changes words on purpose (Ruth 1:5).
Don't neutralize or add gendered language beyond the Hebrew.

## 7. Weights, measures, money

Keep the ancient unit and give a modern equivalent in a note
(*un efa* — about 20 litres).

## 8. Punctuation and typography

- Dialogue in Spanish angle quotes « »; quotations inside them in " ".
- Opening ¿ and ¡ always.
- Verse text has no headings; section headings are a separate layer.

## 9. Notes

Each verse may carry short translator notes:
`alternativa` (another defensible rendering), `texto` (textual variant),
`significado` (meaning of a name or idiom), `medida`. Notes are part of the
published translation and of the review.
