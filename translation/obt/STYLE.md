# Open Bible Translation (English) — Style guide (v0.1, proposed)

The English edition of the Open Bible Translation. Same method as the
Spanish edition (Versión Testigo, `translation/vt/`): drafted from the
Hebrew and Greek, checked, then reviewed and approved by people. Every
decision here is a **proposal** until the review committee signs it off.

## 1. Source texts

- Old Testament: Westminster Leningrad Codex (public domain), word by word
  with Strong's numbers and morphology.
- New Testament: SBL Greek New Testament (CC BY 4.0).
- Follow the Masoretic reading; put ketiv/qere and well-known variants in notes.
- **No published translation is shown to the drafter.** The BSB is used only
  afterwards, as a check.

## 2. Register

- Clear contemporary English that reads well aloud. No archaisms ("behold",
  "thee") and no slang. Contractions are fine in dialogue.
- American spelling.

## 3. How literal

Moderately formal: keep the Hebrew order of ideas, its images and its
deliberate repetitions where natural English allows.

- **Keep key repetitions** (see glossary). In Ruth: *return / go back* (שׁוּב),
  *loyal love* (חֶסֶד), *family redeemer / redeem* (גָּאַל), *wing(s)* (כָּנָף,
  2:12 and 3:9), *worth* (חַיִל, 2:1, 3:11, 4:11), *empty* (רֵיקָם, 1:21, 3:17).
- Keep idioms readers can follow ("spoken to the heart") and explain the rest
  in notes.

## 4. Avoiding copyrighted wording

Modern English Bibles (ESV, NIV, NASB, NKJV, NLT, CSB…) are copyrighted, and
models have memorized them. Write fresh English. Shared wording with the
public-domain KJV, ASV, WEB and BSB is allowed, but don't lean on it either.

## 5. Names of God

| Hebrew | Rendering |
|---|---|
| יהוה (H3068) | **the LORD** (small caps on the web) |
| אֲדֹנָי | Lord |
| אֱלֹהִים (H430) | God / gods |
| שַׁדַּי (H7706) | the Almighty |

## 6. Proper names

Standard English forms: Naomi, Ruth, Orpah, Boaz, Elimelech, Mahlon, Chilion,
Bethlehem, Judah, Moab, Ephrathite(s), Ephrathah, Obed, Jesse, David, Perez,
Tamar, Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Rachel, Leah, Mara.

## 7. People and gender, measures, punctuation, notes

As in the Spanish guide: render by context and don't add or remove gendered
language; keep ancient units with a modern equivalent in a note (an ephah —
about 20 liters / 5 gallons); dialogue in "double quotes" with 'single'
inside; notes typed `alternative`, `text`, `meaning`, `measure`.
